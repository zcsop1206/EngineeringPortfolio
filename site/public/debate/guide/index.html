Rebuttal Board

MIE315. Against the motion "Individual action has more impact on the environment than government policy."

Open the board

The round

For opens (5 min). We open (5). For rebuts (4). We rebut (4). We close (2). For closes (2). The board is built for the nine minutes between the end of their opening and the start of our rebuttal.

Before the round

  1. One person opens the board, presses Session, then New. The code sits after #s- in the address bar. Everyone else opens the same link. Four people, four devices, one log.
  2. Type your first name in the header. Everything you log carries it.
  3. One device holds the Anthropic key: Session, paste, Save. That device answers for the room, so keep it open, plugged in and online. Nobody else enters anything; their header shows model via that person's name once the first answer lands.
  4. Read the Corpus tab once. Our own cards: F1–F6 frames and definitions, M1–M5 measured examples, OP1–OP4 the opening's examples, CL1–CL2 the closing's two pillars. Theirs, with our rebuttals: A/B/C by tier, FP/PL/MK researched by angle.

During their opening

  1. Type each claim and each example as they say it, in their words, and press Enter. Short is fine.
  2. Matching cards appear underneath, best first; the model re-orders them a second later. Tap a card's title for the detail, the sources, and what to say if they push back.
  3. A point no card answers is marked gap and lands in Strategy. On the key device the model researches it at once with web search and attaches a draft with links.

The nine minutes

  1. Gaps. Open Strategy. Each gap shows the research draft if one exists. A teammate opens the links, then presses Links check out or Wrong, drop it. A gap the model could not answer is a teammate's job: press Add a finding and paste what you find, with the link. Copy gaps as text pastes the list into a doc if you prefer working there.
  2. Their case. Tag each point: main claim, load-bearing, weak, or skip. The rebuttal is built from what you tag, not from everything they said.
  3. Counter-claim. Write the one sentence that refutes their main claim. Or steer a proposal first: type a direction in your own words ("lead with scale, concede Montreal, skip recycling"), pick the card to open on and the card to end on, choose how many beats, and leave "keep my roles and counter-claim" ticked so the model fills in only what you have not decided. Propose a strategy then names their thesis, suggests roles, a counter-claim and beats. Apply sets all of it, or apply only the roles, the counter or the beats. Every proposal is kept, so propose again with a different direction and compare.
  4. Rebuttal. Add beats from Their case or press Own beat for a beat in your own words. Press edit on any beat to reword it, pick a different card from the whole corpus, give it a fixed number of seconds, or tick "keep when rebuilding" so an Auto-build or an applied proposal plans around it. Arrows reorder, ⇄ cycles the matching cards, × removes, Undo steps back through earlier versions. Beats without a fixed time share what is left of the four minutes; the view says when you are over. Copy gives plain text with the time marks.
  5. Closing. Auto-build picks a frame that resets the motion, the wins that beat what they argued, and the line to end on. Reorder the same way.

How long research takes

Measured on 2026-11-08 with four claims the corpus did not cover (Danish wind co-ops, The Ocean Cleanup, US beef consumption, Dutch cycling), each researched by a model with web search:

ClaimTimeSources openedConfidence
Danish wind cooperatives44 s2 of 4medium
The Ocean Cleanup45 s1 of 4medium
US beef consumption60 s2 of 4medium
Dutch cycling protests52 s0 of 4low to medium

So: a sourced line arrives in about a minute, but only one or two of its sources were actually opened; the rest are search snippets. The fast path is the model first, a human second: the key device researches every gap as it appears, and a teammate spends their minutes opening the links and pressing the check button, rather than searching from scratch. Reserve human research for the gaps the model marks low confidence, and never read a number aloud from a draft nobody checked.

Reading the marks

  • ? or check source: a source on that card was never fetched by us. Say the point; soften or skip the number.
  • weak: the best answer we have, but a judge may not buy it. Answer briefly and move on.
  • The italic line under a point is the model's link between the point and the first card. It is phrasing, not a fact. The facts are on the cards.
  • your wording on a beat means a teammate rewrote the card's line; the card's own line is still in the copied text. your own words, no card means the beat has no source behind it.

If something breaks

  • The header shows sync: shared means live; offline means keep logging, entries queue and send when the connection returns.
  • keyword only means no model has answered yet. Matching, outlines and the corpus still work. If the key device is closed, outlines build in keyword order after a 20-second wait.
  • Anyone who knows the session code can read and add to the log, so use a fresh code for the real round.
