Rebuttal Board

MIE315. Against the motion "Individual action has more impact on the environment than government policy."

Open the board

The round

For opens (5 min). We open (5). For rebuts (4). We rebut (4). We close (2). For closes (2). The board does two jobs: take down their case while they speak, and build ours in the nine minutes before our rebuttal. The left side is their case. The right side switches between the Corpus and Strategy.

Before the round

  1. One person opens the board, presses Session, then New. The code sits after #s- in the address bar. Everyone else opens the same link. Four people, four devices, one page.
  2. Type your first name in the header. Everything you log carries it.
  3. One device holds the Anthropic key: Session, paste, Save. That device answers for the room, so keep it open, plugged in and online. Nobody else enters anything; their header shows model via that person's name once the first answer lands.
  4. Read the Corpus tab once; 166 cards in twelve groups. First group, their case (TC01–TC12): the claims in the For team's own draft, in their words, with our answers. Then their likely arguments by angle, strongest first within each: consumption, markets, policy fragility, policy failures, movements, legitimacy, communities, norms and ethics (A/B/C, FP/PL/MK, PF/CV/GL/BH). Then ours: frames and definitions (F1–F6, FR, CL), measured policy wins (M1–M5, OW, OP) and the pre-empts for the counters they have already scripted against us (PE1–PE5). Last, fallacies (FL01–FL18): twelve errors to name when they make them, six our own case is exposed to, each with the sentence that closes it.
  5. Open any of their cards and read Other routes: two to four different ways to answer it, labelled deny, reframe, outweigh, turn or fallacy. Pick the route that fits the speech you are giving; the say line is only the default.

During their opening: take down their case

  1. The box at the bottom left is the only thing you type in. Type a point in their words and press Enter. It becomes P1, P2, and so on, in the order they spoke.
  2. Press Tab and the lines that follow go under the last point as its subpoints. Shift+Tab goes back to points. The four words above the box (Point, Sub, Example, Source) show where the next line will land, and tapping one does the same as the keys.
  3. Start a line with eg for an example they gave, or src for a source they cited. A bare link is filed as a source. A line starting with - is a subpoint wherever you are.
  4. Under each line sit the two or three cards that answer it, as a short id and title. Tap one to open it: what to say, the other routes, what they will say back, and the sources. Tap again to close it.
  5. Every line is marked for what it still needs, and the counts sit at the top of the column. needs research: no card answers the line, so there is nothing to say against it yet. source not in corpus: they cited a source no card holds. verify with card ids: a card offered under the line has a source we never fully checked, so check it before you quote its number. On the key device the model reads the cards against each point, subpoint and example. Without it the mark is counted from shared words, so read it as a hint.
  6. Tap any line to select it: you can set or clear the research mark, research it, turn a subpoint into a point, or remove it. Lines you add next go under the selected point.
  7. teammate prepared on a point: it raises a topic your teammate has ready, and its answer goes to them as an offer.
  8. If you have their case as text beforehand, paste it in one go. Each line becomes a point, and indented or dashed lines become its subpoints.

The nine minutes: build ours

Three buttons, used in this order: Suggest, Check, Suggest again, Trim. Suggest proposes. Check edits the content. Trim fits the time. They share one standard for what earns a place, and they remember what you decided, so none of them undoes another.

  1. Main claim. Open Strategy and write the one sentence that refutes theirs. It saves when you leave the box.
  2. Suggest, and take your pick. The first time, it offers other main claims (use) and subclaims for yours, each with its cards and the points of theirs it answers (add). Add the ones you want, or type your own and press Enter. Every suggestion comes from the cards; the model adds no fact.
  3. Examples. Under a subclaim, type in the example line: it searches the cards as you type, Enter attaches the top one, and the last row adds what you typed in your own words. Or open any card on the left or in the Corpus and press the subclaim's name beside Use in. An attached card shows the line to say and its source.
  4. Check. It is an editor for content, not length. Under each subclaim it shows which of their points it answers and where it ranks, and for anything that needs a change it gives one finding with two buttons: do it, or decline it. use a tighter wording or keep mine; merge into the subclaim that makes the same point or keep both; remove one that answers nothing they said or keep it. The Check block holds what concerns the whole plan: a better order, a reworded main claim, and the points their case rests on that nothing answers yet, each with skip it if you choose to leave it. Apply all does every fix at once.
  5. What you decide stays decided. A subclaim you fixed or chose to keep is marked settled, and later checks leave it alone. A finding you declined does not come back. Check again with nothing changed and it says so, without asking the model. Edit a subclaim and only that one is judged again.
  6. Suggest again. Now it proposes a subclaim for each unanswered point first, and nothing you already cut. An answer the check asked for arrives settled.
  7. Trim. Length only, and plain arithmetic: about 150 words a minute, three subclaims in your 3:00, one in your teammate's 1:00 or two short prepared ones, two examples a subclaim. It lists what to detach or cut, from the bottom of the check's ranking, never the only answer to a point their case rests on while something else can go. Apply all, and pressing Trim again finds nothing. The section headings show how much material each of you has, in red when it is over.
  8. Your teammate's minute. Nothing is put into it for them. Suggest proposes one or two subclaims that stand on their own, Check may point at one of yours that would hand over well, and offer to teammate on any of your subclaims sends it across. All of these appear under Teammate as offers with include and decline. Your teammate decides on their own device; a declined offer of yours comes back to you.
  9. Teammate has prepared. The line at the bottom of the Teammate section lists the topics your teammate has ready, separated by semicolons. Card ids after a topic fix which cards go with it. It starts with two: households drive 60 to 72 percent of emissions, and carbon footprint. A topic does nothing until their case raises it. Then Suggest offers its answer to your teammate and not to you, Check names any subclaim of yours on that topic as theirs to take, and Trim hands that one over first. If both topics come up, both are offered, and two short ones fit the minute. The list survives Clear everything.
  10. Research. On the key device a point marked needs research is researched with web search as soon as it appears, and the draft lands under the line with its links; the line then reads verify the draft until someone checks it. A subpoint or example waits for you to select it and press research. Open the links, then press Links check out or Wrong, drop it. Use in on a draft adds it to a subclaim as your own example.
  11. Closing. One box of notes for the two-minute close.
  12. Speaking. Start 4:00 runs a clock everyone in the session sees. It turns red fifteen seconds before 3:00, when you hand over, and again before 4:00. Copy speech gives the whole plan as plain text with the time marks.

With no model reachable all three still work. Suggest and Check fall back to counting matching words and shared cards, and say so; read that ranking as a prompt, not a verdict. Trim never needed a model.

How long research takes

Measured on 2026-11-08 with four claims the corpus did not cover (Danish wind co-ops, The Ocean Cleanup, US beef consumption, Dutch cycling), each researched by a model with web search:

ClaimTimeSources openedConfidence
Danish wind cooperatives44 s2 of 4medium
The Ocean Cleanup45 s1 of 4medium
US beef consumption60 s2 of 4medium
Dutch cycling protests52 s0 of 4low to medium

So: a sourced line arrives in about a minute, but only one or two of its sources were actually opened; the rest are search snippets. The fast path is the model first, a human second: the key device researches every point that needs it as it appears, and a teammate spends their minutes opening the links and pressing the check button, rather than searching from scratch. Reserve human research for the gaps the model marks low confidence, and never read a number aloud from a draft nobody checked.

Reading the marks

  • ?, verify or check source: a source on that card was never fetched by us. Say the point; soften or skip the number.
  • weak: the best answer we have, but a judge may not buy it. Answer briefly and move on.
  • own under a subclaim: an example in your own words, with no card and no checked source behind it.
  • A fallacy card's say line is written to land as reasoning, not as an accusation. Name the error once, then move to the card that answers the point; a judge rewards the answer, not the label.

If something breaks

  • The header shows sync: shared means live. relay down · working locally means the public relay the board syncs through is not answering (it was down for a while on 8 November); offline means this device has no connection. In both cases keep working: everything runs on your device, entries queue, and they send when the relay returns.
  • To catch teammates up while sync is down: Session, Copy session, paste the text in the team chat; each teammate opens Session, pastes it, and presses Merge. Merging twice is harmless. The key device then answers for what it received.
  • Two backups worth setting up before the round. First, share the claude.ai version of the board with teammates by email as Editors; it syncs through claude.ai, not the relay. Second, Session has a relay address field: any ntfy-compatible server works, everyone must be on the same one, and Copy link carries it.
  • Suggest and Check can take up to a minute and a half with the larger model. The button reads Thinking… or Checking… while it runs; if it fails, a message says why and the keyword version appears instead.
  • keyword only means no model has answered yet. The outline, the matching cards, the Corpus and Strategy all still work.
  • Anyone who knows the session code can read and add to the log, so use a fresh code for the real round.
  • To start over, open Session and scroll to Clear everything. The button stays off until you type the session code, so it cannot be pressed by accident. It empties their case, the strategy and the clock for everyone in the session. Nothing is destroyed: Undo clear, in the empty outline and in Session, brings it all back.
